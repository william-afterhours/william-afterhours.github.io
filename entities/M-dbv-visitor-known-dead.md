# Motif: the dead relative who comes for the dying

## 1. One line

A dying person, shortly before death, sees, greets or calls by name a dead relative or friend, often with a gesture of welcome or the words that the visitor has "come for" them. This is the plain structure of DBV (METHOD §1) and the ledger of the DBV **S layer**: it has no verified-information element, because the visitor's death was known. The sibling motif with the V element is [[M-dbv-visitor-unknown-dead]]. In Western Europe the structure is native to Christian deathbed expectation and older than any collection, so for EU-W records the question of independence is only whether the record was borrowed from another cluster (card §11, METHOD §5 note of 2026-10-31).

## 5. Notes

- **Non-Western candidates (none opened):** East Asia — Pure Land "welcoming descent" (raigō 来迎) of Amitābha at the deathbed, a doctrine rather than a case record; whether Japanese or Korean folk records describe a dead relative coming (お迎え) is not investigated. South Asia — the Osis & Haraldsson survey of 1977 (archive.org borrow-only); their journal papers (JASPR 1977) are on archive.org as `sim_` items and should be tried next. Ancient and medieval — Gregory the Great, *Dialogues* IV (6th c.; English translation on archive.org `cu31924029222076`) contains deathbed visions of the dying seeing the dead; a candidate for an ANC-EU entry.
- **East Asia (2026-11-02, card 5).** The structure is in Chinese doctrine ([[T-Dizang-sutra]]) and in Qing anecdote collections, and a dying woman of the eighteenth century quotes it as common knowledge. Two visitor types appear: dead kin (Ji Yun ch. 13, ch. 17) and officials of the other world who escort the dying to a post (Chen Qiyuan 1871; also Wu Weiye's funeral biography of 1672 as quoted by Chen, not opened). Every Chinese record after 1603 has route ② open because Christian books on death were in print in Chinese; every dead-relative record has route ③ open until the sutra's origin is settled. An East Asian I2 needs a record made where Christian books were not accessible (Japan after the seventeenth-century ban, to be verified) and a settled ③.
- **South Asia.** Osis and Haraldsson's Indian survey (JASPR 1977; *At the Hour of Death*, 1977) reports visitors who are often religious figures, "Yamdoot" messengers of Yama (Psi Encyclopedia summary, not a source). **Unverified**: the 1977 JASPR issues are not on archive.org (only the contents and index volumes for 1970–1990), the book is borrow-only, and Haraldsson's archived bibliography gives no full text. Even if opened, the Indian cases share an investigating body with their American cases (route ⑤) → I0.
- **Ancient and medieval Europe.** Gregory the Great's *Dialogues* IV was listed as an ANC-EU candidate. METHOD §5 counts ANC- records separately only when no textual route to another cluster is confirmed; the *Dialogues* stand at the head of the Latin Christian deathbed literature that EU-W records draw on, so they are probably the same lineage as EU-W, not a separate cluster. To be checked before carding.
- **First English access to non-Western deathbed texts** (for route ② of pre-1900 EU-W records): *Buddhist Mahâyâna Texts* (SBE XLIX) 1894; *Garuda Purana* 1908–1911; *Tibetan Book of the Dead* 1927 (archive.org catalogue, 2026-10-31).
