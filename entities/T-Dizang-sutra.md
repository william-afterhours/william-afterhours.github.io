# The Ksitigarbha sutra — the dying see their parents and kin coming to lead them

## 1. One line

A Chinese Buddhist scripture attributed to the Tang translator Śikṣānanda of Khotan. Its chapter 8 says that even people who do good, when their life is ending, see hundreds and thousands of ghosts "changed into parents and other kin" who "lead the dying person away" toward the evil paths. It is the canonical Chinese statement of the deathbed-visitor structure, given as a warning that the visitors may be demons in disguise. This study tracks it because it decides route ③ (religious transmission) for East Asian dead-relative deathbed records: if the sutra is a translation from an Indian original, the idea reached East Asia from South Asia through doctrine; if it was composed in China, it is East Asia's own tradition. **Verdict (2026-11-06): on present evidence composed in China.** Neither Tang catalogue that lists all nineteen works of Śikṣānanda (730, 800) contains it, the CBETA text is collated only against Ming canons, and Indian deathbed doctrine in translation has other visitors (§2). Route ③ for East Asian dead-kin records is therefore ruled out; it reopens only if an Indic or Central Asian source or a pre-Ming witness turns up.
