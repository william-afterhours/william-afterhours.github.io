# Pam Reynolds — singer-songwriter from Atlanta, experiencer of the 1991 "standstill" case

## 1. One line

American musician (1956–2010). In August 1991, aged 35, she had a giant aneurysm of the basilar artery removed at the Barrow Neurological Institute in Phoenix under hypothermic circulatory arrest ("standstill"), and afterwards described an out-of-body view of the operating room, a tunnel, a light, dead relatives and a return. Her account, recorded by the cardiologist Michael Sabom in 1994 and published in 1998, became the most discussed near-death case of the following two decades. Her death is confirmed and the whole published literature uses her real name, so she is named here.

## 5. Notes

- Because she was a musician, her naming the drill's pitch ("a natural D") is the kind of detail a musician would give; it is also the one detail nobody can check.
- In none of her recorded accounts did she mention the clicking sound played into her ears throughout the operation (Woerlee 2011; Smit 2012). Both sides use this: for Woerlee it shows the clicks were not loud enough to notice; for Smit it shows she was not hearing through her ears.
