# Source summary — the JNDS papers on the Pam Reynolds case (so they need not be read again)

Twelve texts from the *Journal of Near-Death Studies* were read in full as UNT IIIF OCR (`tools/unt_ocr.py`) in the session of 2026-10-25. The OCR existed only in the session scratchpad and is not in the repository (© IANDS). All facts used are in `cases/NDE-US-1991-reynolds.md` with source numbers. This page holds the structure of each text, the page references into Sabom 1998 that they give (since that book is not opened), and details not used in the card.

## The primary record, not opened

- Sabom MB. *Light & Death: One Doctor's Fascinating Account of Near-Death Experiences*. Grand Rapids: Zondervan, 1998. The Reynolds account is in chapter 3 (pp. 37–51 per Woerlee 2011, who quotes pp. 37–82) and the discussion at pp. 184–190 (Augustine 2007 cites p. 185 for the femoral cut-downs and p. 187 for the saw). In copyright; no free copy; **unverified**. Everything attributed to it below comes through verbatim quotation in the papers read.

## 2007 exchange — JNDS 25(4)

- **Augustine K.** "Does paranormal perception occur in near-death experiences?" 25(4):203–236, metadc799110. Survey of veridical-perception claims; the Reynolds section gives: age 35, August 1991, body cooled to 60 °F; Figure 1 (p. 218) a timeline of the operation showing that the out-of-body experience began about 2 h 5 min before cooling; the conversation about small vessels corresponds to the "bilateral femoral cut-downs" (Sabom p. 185); anaesthesia awareness about 1–2 per 1,000, 48 % of it auditory; the description of the saw "like an electric toothbrush… a groove at the top" does not fit the Midas Rex instrument (Sabom p. 187), while the "interchangeable blades" in a "socket wrench case" do; her partly shaved head was accurate but learnable afterwards; the first interview was on 11 November 1994. Not used: the other cases surveyed.
- **Sabom MB.** Commentary, 25(4):257–260, metadc799458. Restates the operative record: bypass 10:50; cortical (SSEP) responses flat 11:10; brainstem (BAEP) responses flat 11:24; bypass off, blood drained, aneurysm clipped 11:25; bypass on 11:30, rewarming 65 min; isoflurane 12:05, nitrous oxide 12:50; operation ended about 2:00 PM. Personal communication from S.C., the institute's intraoperative monitoring practitioner (2006-10-10; named in the text, given initials here under the witness rule): moulded speakers in the ears covered with "mounds of tape and gauze… cover the whole ear pinnae"; the conversation about the vessels about 1 h 25 min after induction. Quotes his own disclaimers from 1998 (pp. 49–50, 189–190, 198) and states that his certainty about life after death comes from Christian faith, not from NDE research. Not used: his argument about the continuity of the experience (citing Hastings 2002 p. 94).
- **Tart C.** 25(4):251–256 — **not fetched.**
- **Greyson B.** Commentary, metadc799258 — cites Moody 1999 *The Last Laugh* pp. viii, 8–9; content not transcribed. Nothing used.
- **Augustine K.** "'Does paranormal perception occur in near-death experiences?' defended." 25(4):261–283, metadc799459. Reply; Reynolds at pp. 276–281. Nothing new about the record.

## 2008 letters — JNDS 26(4)

- **Smit RH.** "Further commentary on Pam Reynolds's NDE", 26(4):308–310, metadc937967. Quotes Spetzler's statements in the BBC documentary *The Day I Died* (prod. Kate Broome, 2002): the instruments "were inside their packages"; hearing through the normal pathways, with "clicking modules in each ear", "inconceivable"; "I don't have an explanation for it." Notes that Augustine relied on Woerlee's analysis and gives Woerlee's web page on the case. Signed as editor of *Terugkeer*, Merkawah Foundation / IANDS Netherlands.
- **Sabom MB.** Letter, metadc937968; **Atwater PMH.** Letter, metadc799054 — read; no facts about the record used.

## 2011 exchange — JNDS 30(1)

- **Woerlee GM.** "Could Pam Reynolds hear? A new investigation into the possibility of hearing during this famous near-death experience", 30(1):3–25, metadc461684. Quotes Sabom 1998 pp. 37–82 at length (the "natural D", the "Brrrrrr" of the saw, the woman's voice about the veins and arteries, "Hotel California" during closing; the tunnel, the light and the relatives). Anaesthesia: thiopental induction at 7:15 AM, midazolam, sufentanil, vecuronium, nitrous oxide/oxygen or isoflurane. Brainstem monitoring: clicks at 100 dB, 11.3 per second through moulded earphones taped in place (Sabom p. 41); the 1988 technique paper gives 90–100 dB, 11–33 per second, 100 µs; 40 dB white noise in the other ear as masking. Argues: anaesthesia awareness (1–9 per 1,000); hearing possible through bone conduction and when the clicks are not loud enough to mask speech; Reynolds never mentioned the clicks; the surgical report ends at 2:10 PM with transfer to recovery; the EEG was flat only after the arrest, hours after the perceptions. Addendum: invites readers to reproduce the click stimulus with a tone-generator program.
- **Hameroff S.** Response, 30(1):26–28, metadc461724 — supports the case from his theory of consciousness; nothing about the record.
- **Carter C.** Response, 30(1):29–53, metadc461722. Reprints the key passages of Sabom 1998 pp. 37–47 (used for the verbatim wording in the card); argues that evoked potentials are not conscious perception (pp. 45–46) and that moulded speakers with 100 dB clicks exclude ordinary hearing.
- **Woerlee GM.** Rejoinder, 30(1):54–61, metadc461715. Clarifies that a Midas Rex burr was used first and then a reciprocating saw. Conclusion (p. 60): a physical explanation is "provably possible", the immaterial explanation "unproven", and "the available data are insufficient" to exclude either.

## 2012 letter — JNDS 30(3)

- **Smit RH.** "Failed test of the possibility that Pam Reynolds heard normally during her NDE", 30(3):188–192, metadc938051. Ran Woerlee's proposed test with a tone-generator program: 95–100 dB clicks at 11 per second in one ear and 40–60 dB white noise in the other were intolerable within seconds. Reynolds was "a musician, a singer-songwriter" and never mentioned clicks in four video interviews. E-mail from Carter (2012-06-06) on the purpose of the white noise and the sealing of the speakers; blog posts by Michael Prescott (2011–2012: could hear a television over the clicks; "possibility can't be totally ruled out"); the Amazon discussion board debate with Woerlee (800+ posts since about 2010).
