# Source summary — the four JNDS papers on the "dentures" case (so they need not be read again)

The full texts (OCR or PDF extraction) existed only in the session scratchpad and are not in the repository (copyright). All facts were moved into `cases/NDE-NL-1979-dentures.md` with source numbers. This page holds only the structure of each paper and details not used in the card.

## Smit 2008 — JNDS 27(1):47–61, metadc798921 (direct PDF URL: `/m2/1/high_res_d/vol27-no1-47.pdf`)

- Structure: Lancet quotation (p. 48) → French 2001 and Woerlee 2004 quoted (pp. 48–49) → the van Wees archive obtained in 2007 (p. 50) → two documents (Meijers 1991; Addink 1994-02-03, 12 pages) (p. 50) → excerpts of the 1994 interview set against Woerlee's 2004 claims (pp. 51–56) → recent follow-up: K.B. confirms, telephone interview with T.G., Rivas interview (pp. 56–58) → the patient's later life, no NDE elements (p. 59) → discussion and conclusion (pp. 59–61).
- Details not used in the card: T.G. also said that "now and then" a patient regains consciousness during resuscitation with the cardiac-massage machine (Woerlee 2010 quotes this as a CPR-perfusion argument). The patient's occupation: "steel bender, I believe" (A.A. interview).
- Closing sentence (p. 61): the case "cannot constitute definitive proof of continuation of consciousness, let alone survival of death. But it does provide corroborating testimony that something extraordinary happened" — the author himself disclaims "proof".

## Woerlee 2004 — JNDS 22(4):235–249, metadc799067 (OCR)

- The first half is general physiology of cardiac arrest (oxygen deprivation, CPR efficiency). The dentures case is one paragraph on p. 247, based on the Lancet summary alone: "consciousness through efficient CPR, no pain, the sound of a metal drawer, eyes half open, saw a brother". Smit 2008 rebuts point by point. In 2010 Woerlee turned "no pain" into an explanation of pain "present" (the versions change on the rebutter's side too).

## Woerlee 2010 — JNDS 28(4):181–191, metadc461689 (OCR)

- Structure: introduction (re-analysis after obtaining Rivas 2008 and T.G. 2008) → summary of facts (late 1979, aged 44, Ooij, Canisius, Thumper, 90 minutes, several days in ICU) → ambulance-time calculation (public telephone in 1979, 10–12 km, at least 11 minutes) → physiology of hypothermia (Sealy 1989, Moser 2005, Oberhammer 2008, Mallet 2002) → consciousness during CPR (Lewinter 1989, Bihari & Rajajee 2008, Miller 1961; the "about 20%" is from his own book, Woerlee 2008) → locked-in-like paralysis (Rossen 1943, Laureys 2005) → auditory reconstruction (analogy of the blind) → OBE as a product of hypoxia; his own argument that "a separated consciousness could perceive neither light nor sound" → conclusion: remarkable, but not evidence of survival.
- None of the cited literature opened. Reusable as general grounds for item N.

## Smit & Rivas 2010 — JNDS 28(4):193–205, metadc461736 (OCR)

- Structure: caveat ("30 years ago, records destroyed, T.G. the only witness; a single witness is no guarantee") (pp. 194–195) → ① circumstances of discovery: cardiologists van Lommel and Louridtz consulted, delayed hypothermic brain damage, comparison with the Verdegaal case (pp. 195–197) → ② arrival and first minutes in the CCU: full English translation of T.G. 2008 (dentures out before the Thumper, transfer 5+ minutes, 1+ minute without circulation) (pp. 197–198) → ③ rebuttal of auditory reconstruction: four points + the full Rivas–T.G. exchange (only one cart, tray already pulled out, viewpoint above the cabinet) (pp. 199–202) → ④ pain: "no explanation" admitted, psychogenic/ESP hypotheses, the Kason 2008 simultaneous-viewpoint case (pp. 202–203) → conclusion and critique of the Skeptiko remarks (pp. 203–204).
- Details not used in the card: Smit proposed a "ten blindfolded subjects" experiment to Woerlee by e-mail, without answer. Anny Dirven kept contact with T.G. and searched for further material on B. (result not stated).
