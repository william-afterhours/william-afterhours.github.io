# East Asian deathbed records: route ③ check and search notes

Notes from session 9 (2026-11-06). The session had two aims: to settle route ③ (religious transmission) for East Asian dead-relative deathbed records, and to find a Japanese record for card 6. The first was done and is written into card [[DBV-CN-1793-zhang]] §11, [[T-Dizang-sutra]] and METHOD §5. The second did not produce a card. This file records what was opened, how, and what failed, so that nothing has to be searched for twice.

## 1. Route ③: the Ksitigarbha sutra and Indian deathbed doctrine

**Tang catalogues.** CBETA XML from GitHub:
- `https://raw.githubusercontent.com/cbeta-org/xml-p5/master/T/T55/T55n2154.xml`: 《開元釋教錄》, 730.
- `…/T55n2157.xml`: 《貞元新定釋教目錄》, 800.

Both list 沙門實叉難陀 with 一十九部一百七卷 in the list of Tang translators. Tags and whitespace were stripped and the full text searched. The string 地藏菩薩本願 does not occur in either catalogue, and neither does 地藏本願. Other Ksitigarbha titles do occur: 大乘大集地藏十輪經, 占察善惡業報經 (alias 地藏菩薩經) and 地藏菩薩問法身讚.

**Collation witnesses of Taishō 412.** The CBETA notes in `T13n0412.xml` cite 洪武南藏, 永樂南藏, 北藏 and 嘉興藏, all Ming. There are no Song, Yuan or Palace readings among the CBETA notes. Whether the Koryŏ canon has the text was not checked directly. The Taishō volume's own front matter was not opened.

**Indian deathbed doctrine in translation.**
- 《正法念處經》 卷三十四, Taishō 721, p. 198a19 and p. 200c19. In this text deathbed visions are signs of the next birth: coloured light, a mountain, the future parents. Kin watching at the bed see the dying "grasping at the air". The two passages read 親族兄弟說言病人兩手攬空 and 兄弟親里見之，謂為觸於虛空.
- 《佛說無量壽經》, Taishō 360, p. 268b01, vow 19: 臨壽終時，假令不與大眾圍遶現其人前者，不取正覺.

**CBETA full-text search API (no key):**
- `https://cbdata.dila.edu.tw/stable/search?q=<phrase>&rows=500` gives the works containing the phrase, each with `canon`, `file`, `juan`, `byline` and `time_from`.
- `https://cbdata.dila.edu.tw/stable/search/kwic?work=<T0721>&juan=<n>&q=<phrase>&around=<chars>` gives snippets. It needs `work`.
- `/search/extended` does not accept a `NEAR/n` proximity query (the query is wrapped in quotes). Use phrase queries only.

The card states the route as ruled out and gives the condition for reopening it: an Indic or Central Asian source, or a pre-Ming witness showing that the sutra was translated. Buswell 1990 (*Chinese Buddhist Apocrypha*) remains unopened.

## 2. NDL Lab (Japan): full text per book

- **Search.** `https://lab.ndl.go.jp/dl/api/book/search?keyword=<one phrase>&size=<n>&from=<k>` searches the full text of all books for one phrase. It returns `highlights` (the field is a list in some records and a string in others). The search is very noisy for common words such as 迎ひに來: thousands of hits, mostly ordinary errands.
- **Metadata.** `https://lab.ndl.go.jp/dl/api/book/<pid>` gives a book's metadata and its table of contents (`index`, with image numbers).
- **Full text.** `https://lab.ndl.go.jp/dl/api/book/fulltext-json/<pid>` gives the whole book's OCR, one record per scan image. The script `tools/ndl_fulltext.py <pid> <out_dir>` saves the metadata and a text file with one block per image (`=== p<image>`).
- **The OCR has furigana mixed into the line in Meiji–Taishō typeset books.** Kanji compounds can be split by kana. Patterns must allow a short run of kana between characters.
- Woodblock books (`isClassic`) are also OCR'd, and kanbun phrases can be found with the search.

## 4. Chinese texts before Christian books in Chinese

《夷堅志》 (Hong Mai, 12th century) on Chinese Wikisource has 101 pages, about 650,000 characters, and is incomplete. It was scanned with a regular expression (a deathbed term within 90 characters of a dead-kin or fetching term; session script, not kept). There were 6 windows, none a dead-kin deathbed visitor. CBETA phrase searches limited to works dated before 1584 (見亡妻, 見亡母, 見亡父, 見先亡, 亡母來, 亡父來, 亡妻來) found only Mulian and underworld-tour stories. Neither search produced a usable case.

## 5. Leads for later sessions (not opened, not used)

- **First Catholic catechism in Chinese.** Ruggieri's 《天主實錄》 is usually dated 1584 (Wikipedia, a lead only). This sets the date before which Chinese records have route ② ruled out.
- **Korea.** The date Christian books in Chinese reached Korea. Yi Su-gwang's 《芝峯類說》 (1614) is said to discuss 《天主實義》; not opened.
- **Japan.**
  - The Christian period ran from 1549 to the seventeenth-century bans.
  - The Kan'ei ban on imported Chinese Christian books is usually dated 1630; not opened.
  - Medieval setsuwa (今昔物語集, 発心集, 沙石集) predate 1549.
- **CORT (a different phenomenon).** Katsugorō of Nakano village, whose memories of a previous life were recorded in 1823 by Edo officials and by Hirata Atsutane (《勝五郎再生記聞》). It is a candidate for a CORT card with route ② ruled out by the Christian ban; not opened.

## 6. Session 10 (2026-11-07): China before 1584

Aim: a Chinese record, made before Christian books existed in Chinese (route ② ruled out by date), of a dying person seeing a dead relative or friend, with a named witness (grade C). **Not found.** What was searched, so that it need not be searched again:

Notes and variant readings stripped; `<lb n>` kept for page and line. A scan for a deathbed term within 70–80 characters of a dead-kin, dead-friend or fetching term gave 22 + 55 windows, read by hand.

**Kanripo** (`github.com/kanripo/KR3l0110` … `KR3l0128`, shallow clones; Siku Quanshu text, `<pb:…>` page markers, no punctuation): 宣室志, 唐闕史, 甘澤謠, 開天傳信記, 稽神錄, 江淮異人錄, 茅亭客話, 括異志, **太平廣記 (all 500 juan)**, 分門古今類事, 陶朱新錄, 睽車志, **夷堅志 (the Siku 50-juan text only)**, 博物志, 述異記, 酉陽雜俎, 續幽怪錄, 清異錄, 續博物志. 22 windows.

**CBETA search API** needs the query URL-encoded (a raw Chinese query returns an empty body).

**Results** (all recorded in [[M-dbv-visitor-known-dead]]):
- 627: the monk 道傑 sees his dead friend 樊綽 at his side until death; the friend's daughters dream he has come to fetch him (續高僧傳 ch. 13, T50 p. 530a). Earliest found. **D**: no named witness, 18–38 years later, hagiography.
- 936–947: 張抗 sees his dead grandson in the Pure Land (龍舒淨土文 ch. 5, T47 p. 266a, 1160). D.
- 919 / 1066 / 1187: the wronged dead come for those who wronged them (太平廣記 ch. 124; 括異志 ch. 10; 夷堅志 ch. 6). D.
- A crisis-apparition lead (not DBV): 續高僧傳 ch. 13, after 道傑, the monk 神素 dies at 棲巖寺 on 23rd of the 2nd month, Zhenguan 17 (643); that night 志寬 of 仁壽寺 dreams that 神素 comes to take leave; "at dawn no news of the death was known; soon a messenger came". A named receiver, recorded within about two years. Distance between the two monasteries not checked; nothing written down before the news. **Candidate for a CA card** (would be C at best).

**Not searched:** the full 夷堅志 (420 juan originally; the modern 207-juan edition is not free), 廣異記 and 冥祥記 outside 太平廣記, Ming biji before 1584, Korean records.
